## The question

The Spanish Inquisition is usually remembered as a fanatically Catholic monarchy stamping out heresy. I wanted to know whether that story holds up. Why did Spain, which had lived for centuries with large Jewish and Muslim communities, create its own Inquisition in 1478, and who was it really aimed at?

## What I found

Looking at the events before 1478 and at the Inquisition's own procedural records, I argue that Ferdinand and Isabella took control of the tribunal away from the Papacy and used it as a political instrument. Its main targets were conversos, Jewish families who had converted to Christianity. Through the doctrine of *limpieza de sangre* (purity of blood), the definition of a heretic moved from a matter of belief to a matter of blood.

## Sources

The paper draws on Henry Kamen's *The Spanish Inquisition: A Historical Revision*, Henry Charles Lea's history of the Inquisition, and scholarship on conversos and the 1492 expulsion.

## What it taught me

It's a reminder of how easily legal institutions can be turned to serve prejudice. That's a big part of why I'm interested in policy: who writes the rules, and whom they're really written for.
